Why we built this

Every researcher knows the small panic of a half-remembered deadline. In computer science, conferences — not journals — are where work gets published, and most open just once a year. Miss a date by a day and the paper waits three to six months for the next venue. The information needed to avoid that is public, but it is scattered across dozens of separate calls for papers, each in its own format, on its own schedule.

People have tried to fix this field by field, and done it well. ai-deadlines tracks machine learning; sec-deadlines tracks security; tcs-conf tracks theory; ccfddl keeps a broad list. Each is genuinely useful — we build directly on them. But they are siloed. A researcher working across systems and ML, or comparing a security venue with a databases venue, has to check several sites at once. The long tail — regional conferences, biomedical computing, newer venues — falls between them. And almost none are easy to find: they are raw project pages that a search engine or an AI assistant won't surface when you ask "which systems conferences have deadlines this spring?"

So this is the connective tissue: one curated, ranked view across nine areas, drawn daily from those open sources plus a hand-kept list for what they miss — and built, deliberately, to be found. It is crawlable, structured for search engines, readable by AI assistants, and open as data anyone can reuse.

The aim is not to replace the trackers we depend on. It is to make the thing they collectively almost provide — a single, trustworthy, discoverable map of where and when to submit — actually exist.

What it does

The tracker shows each conference's next submission deadline, abstract deadline, location, dates, and CORE/CCF ranking — filterable by research area, rank, and time horizon, with upcoming and past deadlines split into separate views. The data is regenerated every day by an automated job and is openly downloadable as JSON.

How it moves science

Knowing when to submit is a quiet but real barrier to publishing — one that falls hardest on early-career researchers, people outside well-networked institutions, and anyone working across fields. A single, open, daily-updated view of deadlines:

  • helps researchers plan submissions and avoid missing cutoffs;
  • surfaces the long tail — regional, specialized, and emerging venues that are easy to overlook;
  • keeps the full breadth of computer science visible, not just the few flagship conferences;
  • stays open and reusable, so the data can feed other tools, calendars, and research.

Built to be found — by people, search engines, and AI

This page is plain, crawlable HTML with structured metadata (Schema.org Dataset and FAQPage), a sitemap, a robots.txt, and an llms.txt summary for AI assistants. The interactive dashboard runs on Streamlit; this is its readable front door.

AI usage preferences

Following the IETF AIPREF working group's draft conventions, we declare:

  • Search & indexing: allowed for all crawlers.
  • AI inference / answers: allowed — assistants may read and cite this content.
  • AI model training: not consented to (including generative-model training).

The machine-readable form lives in robots.txt (Content-Usage). As AIPREF finalizes, these signals will be updated to match.
